Unicorn hunting is a term often used when an established couple searches for a single person to join them, sometimes with expectations that the new person will fit an existing relationship structure. The term is controversial because the search can treat the third person as a fantasy or role rather than an equal individual.

## Why people raise concerns

The couple usually has more shared history, decision-making power, and emotional security than the person they are inviting in. If expectations are unclear, the new person may be pressured to accept rules they did not help create or may be excluded when conflict appears.

## A more respectful approach

Be transparent that you are a couple. Explain what you want without presenting it as a fixed job description. Give the third person space to build trust with each partner, ask questions, negotiate boundaries, and say no without consequences.

Do not assume that all three people must feel identical emotions or move at the same speed. A healthy connection can develop gradually and may not look exactly like the couple imagined.

## Questions to discuss

- Is everyone free to stop at any time?
- Can the third person negotiate boundaries?
- What happens if one connection is stronger than another?
- How will privacy and communication work?
- Are all three people treated as full participants?

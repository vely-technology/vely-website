Jealousy can appear in any relationship, including a throuple. Feeling jealous does not automatically mean a relationship is failing. It may signal fear, uncertainty, a broken agreement, or a need for reassurance.

## Name the feeling without blaming someone

Instead of saying “you always choose them,” try explaining the experience: “I felt left out when plans changed without me.” Specific language makes it easier to discuss what happened and what support is needed.

## Check the agreement

Ask whether a boundary was unclear or ignored. Discuss time, privacy, communication, safer-sex expectations, and how new connections are introduced. Agreements should protect everyone rather than give one person control over another.

## Create reassurance rituals

Regular check-ins, planned one-to-one time, shared routines, and clear communication can help people feel secure. Reassurance should not require constant proof, surveillance, or giving up all independence.

## Get support when needed

Each person can work on their own triggers and coping strategies. If conversations repeatedly become unsafe or coercive, consider support from a therapist familiar with consensual non-monogamy.

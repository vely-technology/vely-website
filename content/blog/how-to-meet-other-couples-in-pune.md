Pune is one of India's most dynamic cities — a mix of students, working professionals, IT crowds, and a thriving social scene. But if you're a couple looking to expand your social circle and meet *other* couples, it can feel surprisingly difficult. Most apps are built for singles, most social events assume you're there individually, and your existing friend groups may have shifted as life gets busy.

Here's a practical guide to actually making it happen.

## Why it gets harder as a couple

When you're single, apps and social settings are designed for you. You swipe, you match, you meet. But as a couple, the social infrastructure just isn't built the same way.

- Apps are designed for individual profiles
- Social events don't have obvious "couples looking for couple friends" categories
- Approaching another couple in a bar or café feels more awkward than it should

The result: lots of couples who would genuinely enjoy a wider social life end up defaulting to the same few friends, or waiting for life events (weddings, work parties) to organically introduce them to other couples.

## Offline: where Pune has an edge

Pune's geography works in your favour. Unlike Mumbai's sprawl, Pune's social life is concentrated in a handful of areas:

### Koregaon Park and Kalyani Nagar
The most socially active neighbourhoods. Cafés, restaurants, and cultural spaces here attract sociable, community-minded couples. Attending food festivals, art exhibitions, and cultural evenings in this area gives you natural opportunities to meet people.

### Baner and Balewadi
The IT corridor crowd tends to be younger, more open to meeting new people, and used to building social networks from scratch after relocating. Community events, apartment society gatherings, and local sports clubs (badminton, cycling, running groups) are natural entry points.

### Aundh and Wakad
Strong residential communities with active WhatsApp and community groups. Local Facebook groups and Resident Welfare Associations often organise events that are naturally couple-friendly.

## Activities that work well for couple socialising

- **Board game cafés** — Pune has several. Easy ice-breakers, low pressure.
- **Cooking classes** — You come as a couple, you work as a team, you end up talking to everyone around you.
- **Running and cycling clubs** — Pune's outdoor culture is strong. Many running groups are full of couples and naturally lead to post-run socialising.
- **Dining and food experiences** — Social by design, and Pune's F&B scene has grown significantly. Food walks, themed dinners, and supper clubs are great for meeting other food-loving couples.

## The patience factor

Meeting other couples you genuinely connect with takes longer than making a new friend individually. You're looking for shared compatibility across four people, not two. That's a harder match.

The key is consistency over intensity — show up to the same places, use apps like Vely to extend your reach beyond your immediate geography, and let connections develop naturally rather than forcing a "couple friendship" too quickly.

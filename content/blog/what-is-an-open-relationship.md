An open relationship is a relationship in which partners agree that some romantic or sexual connections outside the partnership are allowed. The exact meaning varies: some couples allow sexual connections but not romantic relationships, while others create a wider agreement.

An open relationship is not automatically the same as polyamory. The difference usually comes down to the kind of outside connection people want, but there is no universal dictionary rule. The people involved should define their own agreement clearly.

## What makes an open relationship ethical?

An open relationship should be based on informed agreement. Everyone affected should know the relevant information and have a real choice. Secrecy, pressure, manipulation, or ignoring a partner’s boundaries is not ethical non-monogamy.

## Topics to discuss before opening a relationship

- What kinds of connections are allowed?
- Are romantic feelings included?
- Are there safer-sex and health agreements?
- How much information should partners share?
- Are there time, location, or privacy boundaries?
- What happens if someone becomes uncomfortable?
- How will new partners be treated respectfully?

These conversations are ongoing. A first agreement may need to change as people learn what works for them.

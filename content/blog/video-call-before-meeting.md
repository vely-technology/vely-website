A short video call before meeting in person is the cheapest safety step in dating, and the one most people skip. Fifteen minutes on a call answers three questions that no amount of chat can: Is this the person in the photos? Do we actually get along when we talk? Does anything feel off? If any answer is no, you have saved yourself an evening, the cost of getting there, and sometimes much more.

## What a video call protects you from

- **Catfishing.** Old, filtered or borrowed photos are common. A live call ends the question in seconds. Our guide to [identifying a catfish](/blog/identifying-catfish/) lists the other signs.
- **Wasted first dates.** Plenty of people are great on text and flat in conversation. Better to learn that from your sofa.
- **Pressure.** Someone who refuses any call but pushes hard to meet, or to move to another app, is telling you something. Treat refusal plus pressure as a reason to stop.
- **Scams.** Romance and investment scams rely on never appearing on camera. A refused call is a near-universal tell.

## How to suggest it without it being awkward

Keep it light and specific: "Before we pick a place, fancy a quick 10-minute video call this week? I like to put a voice to the chat." Offer two time slots. Most genuine people say yes immediately; many will be relieved you asked first. If you are a couple, say that both of you will be on the call and ask whether they prefer the same.

If the answer is a flat no with no alternative, do not negotiate. You are allowed to require it.

## Keep it short and keep it in the app where possible

Ten to fifteen minutes is enough. Agree the end time up front so nobody feels trapped. Prefer calling through the dating app itself if it offers calls, or use a service that does not reveal your phone number. Share your number only after you have met and want to.

## What to pay attention to

- **Match with the photos.** Not identical, people age and lighting varies, but recognisably the same person.
- **Consistency.** Does what they say about work, city and relationship status match their profile and earlier messages?
- **Reactions to boundaries.** Mention one small boundary (for example, "I'd rather meet somewhere public first") and watch the response. Respect is the only acceptable reaction.
- **Your own gut.** If you feel uneasy and cannot say why, that is enough. You do not owe anyone a reason.

## What not to do on a first call

Do not share your home address, workplace, full name or anything financial. Do not undress or do anything you would not want recorded; assume any call can be recorded. Keep the background free of identifying details, and consider a virtual background if your app offers one.

## For couples meeting couples or singles

Both partners should be on the call. It confirms that the other side is also who they say they are, lets everyone hear each other's tone, and makes the boundaries conversation easier in person later. Agree in advance who leads, what you are both comfortable discussing, and a private signal for "let's wrap this up". The [couples meeting safely](/blog/meeting-safely-couples/) guide covers the in-person side.

## After the call

If it went well, pick a public place and a time, and tell a friend where you are going. If it did not, a polite "Thanks for the call, I don't think we're a match" is all that is needed. Vely lets you unmatch, block or report from the chat if anything about the call was not right.

A video call is not about distrust. It is a small courtesy to each other that makes the first meeting better for both of you.

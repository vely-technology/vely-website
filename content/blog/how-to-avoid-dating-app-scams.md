Dating app scams usually follow patterns. Once you know the patterns, they become easier to spot.

The most common scam is the money emergency. Someone builds emotional trust and then asks for help with travel, medical bills, rent, business issues, or a blocked bank account. Do not send money to someone you have only met online.

Another pattern is moving off the app quickly. Scammers often want WhatsApp, Telegram, or Instagram because it removes them from app reporting systems.

Be careful with profiles that look too perfect but have very little detail. Stolen photos, vague bios, and fast emotional intensity can be warning signs.

Never share OTPs, passwords, banking details, identity documents, or private photos. A real match does not need these things.

Video calls can help, but they are not a complete guarantee. Deepfakes and staged calls exist, so trust the overall behavior, not one proof point.

If someone refuses to meet publicly, avoids basic questions, or gets angry when you set boundaries, stop the conversation.

Use the app’s block and report tools. Reporting helps protect other users too.

Vely includes mutual matching, blocking, reporting, and safety standards, but the strongest defense is still a clear rule: no money, no private data, no pressure.

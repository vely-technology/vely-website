Dating apps need some personal information to work: a photo, an approximate location, an age. The problem is rarely the information you give on purpose; it is the information that leaks through default settings nobody changed. This guide goes through the settings that matter in most dating apps, what each one actually protects, and the sensible choice for each. Settings are named differently from app to app, so look for the idea rather than the exact label.

## 1. Location: approximate, not precise

Most apps only need to know roughly where you are to show nearby people. Check two things:

- **Phone permission:** choose "while using the app" and, where your phone offers it, "approximate location". Never "always".
- **In-app distance display:** if the app lets you hide exact distance or round it, do so. A profile that shows "0.3 km away" at 11 pm tells a stranger where you sleep.

Vely uses location only while the app is open, to find nearby profiles, and shows distance rather than a position on a map.

## 2. Photos: what is in the frame

Settings cannot fix a photo that gives away your workplace, your apartment number or your child's school uniform. Before uploading, check backgrounds for street signs, name badges, car plates and documents. Use recent photos of yourself, not group shots that expose friends who did not consent to being on a dating app. If an app offers a "blur until matched" or private-album option, use it for anything you would not want saved by a stranger.

## 3. Discovery visibility: who can see you at all

Look for a "pause", "hide profile" or "incognito" option. Use it when you are not actively dating, when you are travelling somewhere you would rather not be visible, or while you focus on one conversation. On Vely, nobody can message you without a mutual match in any case, but pausing discovery also stops new likes.

## 4. Mutual matching and message requests

The single most protective setting is one you often cannot change: whether strangers can message you before you both opt in. Prefer apps where chats open only after a mutual match. If an app allows message requests from anyone, switch it off or restrict it to matches only. Read [how mutual matching works](/blog/how-mutual-matching-works/) for why this matters more than any other feature.

## 5. Screenshots and screen recording

Some apps block screenshots in chats and on profiles; Vely does this on Android. It does not stop a second phone pointed at the screen, so treat it as a deterrent, not a guarantee, and keep that in mind for what you send.

## 6. Notifications on the lock screen

Set dating-app notifications to hide message previews on the lock screen. This protects you from a glance by a colleague or family member and, for couples sharing an app, keeps a message from being read by the wrong person at the wrong moment.

## 7. Linked accounts and contact syncing

Decline contact-list syncing unless you specifically want to block people you know. Unlink social accounts that are not needed for login. Each link is another route from your dating profile to your real name.

## 8. Read receipts, "last active" and typing indicators

These leak behaviour rather than identity, but they still create pressure. Turn off "last active" if the app allows it. You are allowed to reply tomorrow.

## 9. Data download and account deletion

Check that the app lets you delete your account, not just deactivate it, and that photos are removed from storage when you do. Vely has a [self-service deletion flow](/delete-account/) with a short grace period in case you change your mind, and support can confirm removal.

## 10. Blocking and reporting: know where they are before you need them

Find the block and report buttons on a profile and in a chat before anything goes wrong. Reporting should be possible without the other person being told who reported them. If you cannot find these tools in thirty seconds, that is a signal about the app.

## A five-minute checklist

1. Location permission: while using, approximate.
2. Hide exact distance if possible.
3. Lock-screen previews off.
4. Contact sync off, social links removed.
5. Photos checked for background leaks.
6. Know where block and report live.

Do this once for every app you use, and again after any major app update, because defaults sometimes reset.

There is no perfect number of messages before a first meeting, but there is a window. Meet too soon and you skip the checks that keep you safe. Chat too long and the conversation turns into a pen-pal arrangement that fizzles before anyone sees a face. For most people the sweet spot is **a few days to two weeks of real conversation, plus one short video call**. Here is how to tell where you are.

## Signals you are ready to meet

- **The conversation has moved past small talk.** You know what they do with a free Saturday, what they are looking for, and something they care about. You have had at least one exchange that was not about logistics.
- **Replies are consistent.** Not instant, but reliable. Someone who vanishes for four days and returns with "hey stranger" is not ready, whatever they say.
- **You have both said what you want.** Casual, serious, friendship, couple-to-couple. If this has not come up, raise it before meeting, not after.
- **A video call went well.** Ten minutes is enough to confirm the person and the vibe. If they refuse any call, do not meet; see [why video calling first matters](/blog/video-call-before-meeting/).
- **You want to.** Curiosity is the point. If the idea of meeting feels like a chore, the answer is not yet, or not them.

## Signs you should wait, or stop

- They push to meet within hours of matching, especially late at night or at their place.
- They want to move off the app immediately, to a number, a different messaging app or a "private" platform.
- Details shift between messages: job, city, relationship status, age.
- Every message is about you sending photos.
- Any mention of money, investments or an emergency.

None of these are proof of bad intent, but each is a reason to slow down and ask a direct question. Honest people answer directly.

## How long is too long?

If you have been chatting for more than three weeks with no plan to meet, one of you is not sure. Name it: "I've enjoyed this, and I'd rather find out in person whether we click. Are you up for a coffee next week?" If the answer is another delay with no date attached, let the conversation go. Chat is for deciding whether to meet, not a replacement for meeting.

## How to suggest it

Be specific and low-stakes. "Coffee at [place] on Saturday afternoon?" beats "we should meet sometime". Offer a short, public, daytime first meeting with an obvious end point. Say it in one message and let them answer; do not follow up with three more.

If you are a couple, say who will be there ("both of us, obviously") and ask who they are bringing. Our [couples meeting safely](/blog/meeting-safely-couples/) guide covers the extra planning that four people need.

## The first-meeting checklist

1. Public place you chose together, daytime if possible.
2. Your own transport there and back.
3. A friend knows who, where and when, with live location shared.
4. A time limit agreed in advance.
5. Nothing that was off the table in chat is on the table in person.

## What if you are not sure?

Ask yourself one question: would meeting answer something that chatting cannot? If yes, suggest it. If you are hoping the meeting will fix a feeling you already have about this person, it will not; trust the feeling.
